Many helpers, many people, one truth.

One Alfred is a shared record that lets everyone working on the foundation's projects tune in to the same picture of the work.

One Alfred, many Alfreds

Every helper that sits down to work, human or AI, is an Alfred for an hour: it arrives, does its part and leaves. One Alfred is what they share: one record of who is doing what, what changed and what is waiting on whom, so that nobody starts cold and nobody works over someone else.

Nobody owns the truth. Each person and helper keeps their own place in it, asks what changed since they last looked, and adds what they did. People decide; the record remembers.

The idea in five lines

  • Awareness and collisions first, automatic dispatch last. The first two need no new billing and no model running by itself.
  • One record per kind of fact. Code stays in git, documents stay in Drive, and a small append-only log records who is doing what.
  • Tune in like git fetch. Each person or helper asks what changed since they last looked. Nothing is pushed into a session in the middle of deep work.
  • The machine queues; people decide. A finished task frees its locks and queues an independent review in one step. No helper can mark work accepted.
  • Pointers, not bodies. The log holds links, hashes and short notes. It has no place for personal material, so such material has nowhere to be filed.

Three zones

What belongs to a person stays with that person. Only pointers move into the shared zone.

Where it stands

Each stage stops by itself unless a person approves the next.

  1. Contractwe are hereA shared shape for tasks and results, used by hand. Alongside it, a read-only index and a log of what changed, run by a person.
  2. Shadow ledgerThe log runs beside the foundation's existing document, which stays the record.
  3. Notify"N new for you" reaches the right person, without anyone relaying it.
  4. CutoverThe log becomes the record, only when the people responsible say so.
  5. DispatchQueued work starts helpers by itself, under spending caps, once money and authority are decided.
